# Product context

Keep this file short. Omit any section that has no real content.

## What the app is

One or two sentences: what it does and in what setting.

## Who uses it

- Primary users, their role, and technical level.
- Where and on which device they use it.

## Main tasks

The few things users do most often or must finish fastest, in priority order.

## UI and UX priorities

- Components and patterns to favor (for example tables, inputs, detail modals).
- Information density, readability, and language or terminology expectations.

## Not a priority

What the app is not for, so agents do not drift into it (for example marketing pages, heavy animation).

## Decision rules for agents

- When unsure, prefer X over Y, because the users need Z.

## Terms

Domain words and what they mean here.

---

## Example: internal tool

- **What:** Back-office app for the support team to review and update customer orders.
- **Users:** Support staff on desktop, all day, comfortable with dense screens.
- **Tasks:** Find an order, read its details, change status.
- **Priorities:** Tables with filters, plain inputs, detail modals that are easy to read.
- **Not a priority:** Marketing visuals, onboarding tours.
- **Rules:** Prefer a clear table over cards. Prefer showing all order fields over hiding them behind extra clicks.

## Example: public app

- **What:** Mobile-first booking site for first-time visitors.
- **Users:** Anonymous visitors on phones, low patience, no training.
- **Tasks:** Pick a slot and confirm in under a minute.
- **Priorities:** Large tap targets, few steps, plain language.
- **Not a priority:** Admin-style dense tables.
- **Rules:** Prefer one question per screen over a long form. Prefer defaults over extra options.
